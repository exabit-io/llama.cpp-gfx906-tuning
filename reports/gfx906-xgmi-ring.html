Measurement report · Mac Pro (2019) · 2026-09-04

Four Vega 20 dies, one XGMI ring

Two Radeon Pro Vega II Duo modules bridged with Apple's Infinity Fabric Link connector, measured under Linux and ROCm: four gfx906 dies in a single hive, four full-duplex links, all of them loaded at once.

The claim under test: "gfx906 was never capable of a 4-way XGMI ring topology." Everything below is a measurement from a machine that runs one.

1 · What the vendors say

Four GPUs in a hive ring is the documented configuration

"With two Infinity Fabric links per GPU, they can deliver up to 200 GB/s of peer-to-peer bandwidth" and "you can configure up to four GPUs in a hive ring configuration, made of two hives in eight GPU servers."

AMD's Radeon Instinct MI50/MI60 announcement (Vega 20, gfx906), as reported by PC Perspective, November 2018

"Onboard Infinity Fabric Link connection connects the two Vega II GPUs at up to 84GB/s" (Vega II Duo). "Infinity Fabric Link connection enables two Vega II GPUs to connect at up to 84GB/s" (Vega II).

Apple, Mac Pro (2019) technical specifications. 84 GB/s is bidirectional: 42 GB/s each way per link.
2 · What the driver reports

One hive of four, with a ring-shaped hop matrix

amdgpu publishes the hive membership and the PSP's hop counts in sysfs; KFD turns them into link weights (15 = one XGMI hop, 30 = two) and rocm-smi --showtopo shows XGMI for every pair. Every die reports two one-hop peers and one two-hop peer. With two XGMI links per Vega 20 die, the only four-node shape that gives that matrix is a ring. The hive's VRAM sits in one flat address space, base 0x80_0000_0000 + node × 32 GiB.

3 · The ring, measured

Four direct links at 507 ns, two 2-hop pairs at 618 ns

A single thread chases a pointer chain through another die's memory (4160-byte stride, 20 000 dependent loads), so each number is the round trip of one read. Rows are the die doing the reading, columns own the memory. HIP device 0 is 0b:00.0, 1 is 0e:00.0, 2 is 1b:00.0, 3 is 1e:00.0; 0b and 0e share one MPX module (Slot-1), 1b and 1e the other (Slot-3).

The latency matrix, the contention test in section 5 and the saturation test in section 4 all agree on which pairs are wired together, and they disagree with the firmware. The PSP's XGMI trusted application (queried by amdgpu with GET_TOPOLOGY_INFO) says the bridge joins 0b to 1e and 0e to 1b. It joins 0b to 1b and 0e to 1e: the straight pairing, die A to die A, die B to die B.

4 · Saturation

Four links, eight directions, 256 GB/s at the same time

Copy-engine (SDMA) flows of 1 GiB, each pinned to one of the die's two SDMA engines with hsa_amd_memory_async_copy_on_engine, all released together and timed on the host clock until the last one finishes; best of five. 5 · Two hops share the ring

The 2-hop pairs ride on the same four links

If the 2-hop pairs had links of their own, their traffic would add to the ring's. It doesn't. The four 2-hop flows alone reach 116.4 GB/s; a one-way ring alone reaches 135.5; both together reach 132.3, not 251.9. The per-flow numbers say exactly where each 2-hop flow went.

6 · All-reduce

A tensor-parallel all-reduce across the four dies

fp32 sum of 1 GiB per device, ring reduce-scatter followed by ring all-gather. Every run below was checked element by element and came back exact. busbw is the nccl-tests convention, algbw × 2(N−1)/N; on a ring it is the per-link, per-direction rate the collective actually sustained.

44.7 GB/s busbw over two rings means each link carried about 22 GB/s each way for the whole collective, two-thirds of what a lone flow gets. Telling RCCL the real topology is a 35% gain on its own.

7 · A caveat about shader traffic

Copy engines saturate the fabric; kernels don't, in some patterns

RCCL moves data with kernels reading and writing peer memory. On this fabric that path has failure modes the copy engines don't have: both directions of a 2-hop pair at once fall to 0.62× a single flow (shader writes) where the copy engine gets 1.99×; a one-directional ring of four shader-write flows totals 34.7 GB/s where the copy engine gets 135; the full both-ways ring reaches 150–205 GB/s from kernels against 256 from the engines, and does best with only 8–32 workgroups per flow.

Link latency barely moves under load (506 → 563 ns), so the links aren't the limit; it is how the shader memory path and the fabric interact under cyclic load on Vega 20. The fabric itself delivers 44.7 GB/s busbw to a ring all-reduce and 256 GB/s to raw traffic.

It is not a clock effect either. The whole suite was first run with the core clock clamped at 1000 MHz; unclamping it to 1730 MHz took 100–120 ns off every latency and moved no bandwidth by more than 1%, the shader shortfalls included (one-way ring of shader writes 33.8 → 34.7 GB/s, 2-hop pair both ways 0.61× → 0.62×, RCCL 23.8 → 24.8 busbw).

8 · Reproduce

Everything here comes from four commands

/root/xgmi_map.sh                                     # driver's view: hive, hops, VRAM windows, KFD, rocm-smi
/root/build_ifl_allreduce.sh && /root/ifl_allreduce -b 1 -r 5      # latency, saturation, 2-hop, all-reduce
/root/ifl_allreduce -b 1 -r 5 --ring driver           # the same tests on the ring the firmware claims
cd /root/rccl-tests/build && ./all_reduce_perf -b 1M -e 1G -f 2 -g 4 -n 20 -w 5 -c 1
NCCL_TOPO_FILE=/root/rccl_topo_fixed.xml ./all_reduce_perf -b 1M -e 1G -f 2 -g 4 -n 20 -w 5 -c 1

One thing to know before reproducing: in the first two GPU processes after this boot, the probe saw one direction of every link at 2.4 GB/s (0b→0e, 1b→0b, 1e→0e, 1e→1b, and the 2-hop paths that use them) while the opposite directions ran at 31, for copy-engine and shader reads alike. It cleared within two minutes, before the all-reduce benchmark ran, and has not recurred. amdgpu on this kernel has XGMI power-state switching stubbed out, so the link state after boot is whatever the firmware leaves; run the probe twice after a boot before measuring.
