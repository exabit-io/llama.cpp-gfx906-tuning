# 2026-11-27: llama.cpp v1.1.0 on gfx906, part 2 - the timing: following upstream against mxxm-t's fork, the tile rule, a decode penalty at 8 and 16 slots found in our own commits, the acceptance and the release

Part 1 is `2026-11-26-v110-rebase.md` (the merge, the port of the Exabit commits, correctness). Its timing was void:
the GPU clocks had latched at 1000 MHz. The box was rebooted on 2026-11-27 01:32 UTC, the latch was gone, and every
number below is from that boot at full clock. Record with every step: `/root/rebase-v110/README.md` on the box;
scripts in `tools/release/v110/`, raw result files in `data/raw/2026-11-26/v110/`.

All runs: macpro2019-01, 4x gfx906 (Vega 20), kernel 7.0.0-43, GPUs at 125 W by macpro-fand 2.0.4, Qwen3.8-27B Q8_0,
`-sm tensor`, f16 KV, RCCL built in. Each session runs its arms in blocks, every block every arm once in a fresh random
order; arms are compared within a session only. Statistic where an interval is given: samples paired by block, mean of
ln(a/b), Student's t, 95 % interval, Holm's correction over the rows of a pair. Every watchdog log's clock column was
read after its session: no latch in any (the longest stretch of a loaded die at 1000 MHz was 25 s, never all four).

his fork. "Major" was fixed before the run as: significant after Holm AND the whole 95 % interval beyond 2 % (the
acceptance floor of every package so far).

## 3. The LLVM 24 rule for the head-256 tile kernel on v1.1.0: 88

clean: the `<8,1>` kernel reloads 19 values per loop iteration. 88 and 75 leave no reload in any of the 17 kernels
(part 1, section 5). A count is not a rate, so the candidates were timed, each as the ROCm 10.1 build of master with
only the tile object exchanged.

## 4. Where the 8- and 16-slot decode penalty comes from

Session `attr8` (`attr8.sh`, 12:59-14:10 UTC, 63 of 63 runs, n = 3): master's 22 commits cut into six groups along
its history, one build per cut, decode per slot of each group against the one before:

| Step | Group | 8 x 4096 | 16 x 2048 | 4 x 4096 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a1 / a0 | RCCL default, all-reduce size gate and its default, FA_QUANTS=all | **-12.37 % [-12.76, -11.99]** | **-2.07 % [-2.28, -1.86]** | +0.06 % (n.s.) |
| a2 / a1 | the folds (terms 02 03 04 07 08 10 12) | +1.44 % | -0.18 % (n.s.) | +0.94 % (n.s.) |
| a3 / a2 | DPP warp reductions (terms 23 28) | +0.14 % (n.s.) | +0.11 % | +1.04 % |
| a4 / a3 | MMVQ (terms 01 15 05 09) | +1.80 % | **-1.92 % [-2.07, -1.77]** | +0.66 % (n.s.) |
| a5 / a4 | max-ilp scheduler | +4.50 % | +0.66 % | -0.04 % (n.s.) |
| a6 / a5 | tile rules, GDN fold exclusion | -0.03 % (n.s.) | -0.02 % (n.s.) | +0.11 % (n.s.) |
| a6 / a0 | all 22 | **-5.33 % [-5.63, -5.03]** | **-3.40 % [-3.63, -3.17]** | +2.80 % |

The finding repeated (the first session had -5.25 % and -3.24 %), and the steps add up exactly. Two carriers:

**The all-reduce gate (a1), 12 % at 8 slots.** The logs show it without a further run: every merge log at 8 and 16
slots carries `TP custom AllReduce: auto-selected two-shot for 4-rank decode-sized tensors`, no master log does.
The fork sends every message under 262144 elements (51 rows of 5120) through its custom all-reduce, which since his
commit `19d784ad0` (2026-09-18) uses a two-shot kernel from 8192 elements at four ranks. Our `GGML_TP_AR_MAX_NE`
default of 20481 sends everything wider than 4 rows to RCCL. It was set in September from two measurements that no
longer hold: the custom path "loses at 8-16 rows (-8 %)", measured on the broadcast kernel before the two-shot
commit; and "-19 % prefill ungated", measured on builds without RCCL, where the fork's code applies no threshold at
all. Every acceptance since ran at 1 and 4 slots, where the gate changes nothing, so the cost was never seen.

**The MMVQ window (a4), 2 % at 16 slots.** Of the group's commits only term 01 acts at 16 columns
(`MMVQ_MAX_BATCH_SIZE` 8 -> 16: MMVQ in place of MMQ for 9 to 16 columns). At 16 columns MMQ's tile is full and MMVQ
pays per column. The same group is a gain at 8 and at 4 columns.

The other groups are gains at these widths as well; max-ilp alone is +4.5 % decode at 8 slots and +2.1 % prefill
everywhere.

By the rules written into the script before its first run:

- **The gate is the mechanism** (c against r repeats section 4's first step with the sign turned), and **the custom
  path pays up to 16 rows and costs from 24**. New default: `GGML_TP_AR_MAX_NE` = 81921 (16 rows of 5120, plus one).
  17 to 23 rows were not timed and go to RCCL. Neither old setting was right: ours (4 rows) gave away 14 % at 8
  slots, the fork's (51 rows) gives away 2 to 5 % at 24 to 48.
- **The gain is the two-shot kernel's**, which the fork selects for small messages at four ranks and more only. The
  new default therefore applies at four ranks and more; two and three ranks keep 20481.
- **MMQ is ahead of MMVQ at every width timed from 10 to 16 columns** (1.0 % rising to 1.8 %). The limit goes back to
  8, upstream's value; the launch knobs and the Q8_0 fast path of the same original commit act at 1 to 8 columns and
  stay. September's result (stock b10288: MMVQ ahead at 9 to 16) does not hold on this tree. With the limit at 8,
  master is ahead of the merge at all five widths (last column).
- Prefill of c against r: -0.6 to -0.9 % at 8, 12, 16 slots, level at 24 to 48; in seconds a constant 0.15 to 0.18 per
  run. Section 6 tests what it is.
- c against a0 at 48 x 512 is -1.93 % [-2.62, -1.24]: with the custom path at 48 rows our commits cost 2 % against the
  merge. What ships does not run that combination (48 rows go to RCCL, where master is 3.3 % ahead of the merge), so
  the carrier is named and was not looked for. Whoever widens the gate past 16 rows finds it first.

The candidate on existing builds (m up to 16 rows, r from 24) against master as shipped: **+14.3 % decode at 8 slots,
+7.0 % [+6.96, +7.06] at 12, +3.8 % [+3.49, +4.03] at 16**, unchanged from 24.

## 6. Two loose ends of the new gate

Session `gate9` (`gate9.sh`, 16:42-17:39 UTC, 42 of 42 runs).

**The prefill difference is the benchmark's warm-up batch.** `llama-batched-bench` decodes 16 tokens before the timed
prefill. Under the old gate that batch went through RCCL; under the new one it takes the custom path, and RCCL's
first call falls into the timed prefill. Test at 12 x 2048, five blocks, one build, three gates: 20481; 81921 (the
new default); 81920 (one element less: the 16-row warm-up batch back on RCCL, decode at 12 rows still custom).

|---|---|---|
| 20481 | 12.4028 | 1170.29 |
| 81921 | 13.1283 | 1159.90 |

81920 against 20481 in prefill: -0.03 % [-0.29, +0.24]. 81921 against 81920: -0.86 % [-1.06, -0.67], 0.18 s per run.
One first call per process; no prefill message changes path, and a server pays it once after start.
Decode of 81921 against 20481: +5.85 % [+5.71, +5.99].

**A build without RCCL gets the same default.** There everything over the gate takes upstream's internal all-reduce.
81921 against 20481 in decode per slot: 8 x 4096 **+28.35 % [+27.91, +28.79]**, 16 x 2048 **+12.16 % [+11.90,
+12.42]**, 24 x 1024 level. With no gate at all prefill loses 24 % at all three widths (September's finding stands)
and decode at 24 rows gains another 9.0 %: such a build would want a gate wider than 16 rows. Not pursued; the tree
builds with RCCL by default and no package is built without it.

**What went onto master** (local, final hash `c782c317d`, 26 commits over the merge): `MMVQ_MAX_BATCH_SIZE` 16 -> 8,
and `GGML_TP_AR_MAX_NE` unset = 81921 at four ranks and more (two and three ranks keep 20481, where the two-shot
kernel does not run and nothing new was measured). `GGML_TP_AR_MAX_NE=<n>` and `=0` behave as before.

`firstreq-v110.sh` (four arms x three server starts x twelve requests): the first request of a new server takes
0.13 s longer than later ones in every arm (package, source builds on both runtimes, v1.0.0); no later request was
slow. So the slow starts seen in the package test, and the one in v1.0.0's data, are episodes of the pauses and not
a property of the ROCm 10.1 runtime or the package. `HIP_ENABLE_DEFERRED_LOADING=0` cannot be used as a test: no
program starts with it.

KFD keeps a counter of the time a process's queues were evicted (`evicted_ms`). Logged once a second
(`evsample.sh`): every `llama-batched-bench` process has one eviction of 0.7 s (sum over four dies) during the model
load; a server has about 150 ms in every request from the third on. `kfdtrace-v110.sh` put the kernel's function
tracer, with stacks, on the three eviction paths for one server: all of it is `svm_range_evict`, entered from the
server's own `munmap` (102 times) and `madvise` (16) and from the runtime's SVM migration during the load (40); the
buffer-move path has no entry. Each stops every queue of the process until a kernel worker restores them: 275 ms once
in the load, 34 to 40 ms once per request, with under 10 ms of visible cost per request. The HIP runtime registers
host memory as SVM ranges; when the program unmaps memory in or within 2 MB of one, the driver evicts, because gfx906
cannot retry a GPU page fault. This is a way a program's own memory handling stops all four dies with the host idle.
That the evening's pauses are this path is not shown: none occurred under the trace. `NEXT-STEPS.md` has the
procedure for the next occurrence and the levers.
